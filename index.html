One of the Greatest Video Games of All Time

When Naughty Dog released The Last of Us in 2013, it completely shifted expectations for what video games could achieve, blending cinematic storytelling with intense, grounded survival gameplay.

Here is what makes it so special:

  • 1. Unforgettable Character Dynamics

    At its core, the game isn't actually about the infected; it's about human connection in a broken world. The bond between Joel (a hardened, grieving smuggler) and Ellie (a fierce, immune 14-year-old girl) evolves organically across four seasons. Their relationship feels earned, moving from reluctant partnership to deep paternal love.

  • 2. Relentless Atmosphere and Tension

    Unlike power-fantasy games where you massacre hordes of enemies effortlessly, The Last of Us makes every resource matter.

    • Scarcity: Finding three bullets or a single rag feels like a massive victory.
    • Stealth & Horror: The sound design — particularly the chilling, clicking vocalizations of the Clickers — creates an incredible sense of dread.
    • Grounded Combat: Fights feel visceral, desperate, and dangerous.

  • 3. Moral Ambiguity over Clichés

    There are no clear-cut heroes or cartoonish villains. Every faction you encounter is simply trying to survive, leading to morally gray decisions that leave a lasting impression long after the credits roll. The ending, in particular, remains one of the most debated and emotionally devastating climaxes in gaming history.

  • 4. A Rare Adaptation Success

    The story proved so strong that HBO adapted it in 2023 into a critically acclaimed television series starring Pedro Pascal and Bella Ramsey. It broke the longstanding "video game adaptation curse," earning multiple Emmys and proving that the story's emotional weight translates effortlessly across media.

Controversial Sequel

When Naughty Dog released The Last of Us Part II in 2020, it pushed the boundaries of the medium even further by doubling down on structural complexity, technical fidelity, and relentless mechanical refinement.

However, the sequel was met with a polarizing reception. While some praised its bold narrative choices and technical achievements, others criticized its story direction and character arcs. The game sparked intense debates about storytelling in video games, the treatment of beloved characters, and the expectations of sequels in established franchises.

Key Drivers of the Controversy:

  • Major Story Leaks

    Weeks before release in 2020, massive plot points and cutscenes leaked online. Out of context, these major plot twists sparked widespread outrage, review bombing, and heated social media culture wars before players even had the game in hand.

  • The Fate of Joel

    Early in the campaign, Joel is brutally executed by a new character named Abby. For players who spent years forming an emotional connection with Joel, seeing him killed off so early — and in such a unceremonious fashion — felt like a slap in the face.

  • Forcing the Player's Perspective

    Midway through Ellie's quest for revenge, the game abruptly halts and forces you to play as Abby for nearly 10–15 hours. You experience her backstory, her motivations (her father was the Firefly doctor Joel killed at the end of Part I), and her humanity. This structural gamble worked brilliantly for some, but alienated others who refused to empathize with Joel's killer.

  • Ludonarrative Dissonance & Exhaustion

    The game's primary message is that "revenge destroys everything and everyone involved". However, to progress the story, players are required to commit brutal acts of violence, making the narrative feel unrelenting, bleak, and at times emotionally draining.

  • Culture War Reactions

    The game introduced diverse characters, including Ellie's partner Dina, a muscular female protagonist in Abby, and a young trans boy named Lev. Unfortunately, this triggered bad-faith backlash and harassment online regarding character identities.
